Project 0: Becoming Friends with Your Camera

The point of this project was to see that perspective comes from where you stand, not from how much you zoom. Zooming only changes how much of the picture you keep.

1. Selfie, close vs. far

Both photos have my face at about the same size, but the close one looks wrong. My nose is much bigger and my ears almost disappear. That happens because up close my nose is way nearer to the camera than my ears are, so it gets projected larger. When I back up, everything on my face is roughly the same distance away, so the proportions look normal again. The lens is not the problem, the distance is.

2. Building, near vs. far

Same idea in reverse. From far away the arches stack up on each other and the whole building looks flat, almost like a poster. When I walk up close, the near arch towers over the far one and you can actually feel how deep the hallway is. People call this telephoto compression, but the lens is not doing it. Standing far away is.

3. Dolly zoom

I took five photos while walking backwards and zooming in, keeping my head the same size in every frame. The size stays locked, but the shape does not. In the first frame the camera is right in my face so my nose bulges out, and by the last frame I am far enough back that my face goes almost flat. It is the same thing as part 1, just spread out into an animation.
